# Stage 1. Study concept specification

Use this specification for Stage 1 of [Behavioral Experiment Workflow.md](<../Behavioral Experiment Workflow.md>) to develop the researcher's initial idea into CONCEPT.md. This document establishes the study's purpose, proposed participant activities, intended observations, population, execution setting, and practical constraints. It supplies the starting point for DESIGN.md.

<goal>

Describe what the study aims to investigate or assess, what participants are expected to do, and how the intended observations relate to the research purpose.

Keep the document at the conceptual level. Detailed procedures, exact operational rules, and the technical implementation are developed in the subsequent stages. Preserve specific requirements already established by the researcher so that they can inform the design.

</goal>

<response_requirement>

## 1. Establish the research purpose

Use the researcher's stated question, objectives, background, and supplied references. Explain the concepts or behaviors relevant to the study in the researcher's intended terms. Include hypotheses or expected relationships when the researcher has established them.

Distinguish the observations the study seeks to obtain from predictions about what those observations will show. Verify factual claims and references when developing the background; do not invent supporting literature or findings.

## 2. Describe the proposed study

Identify the intended participant population, proposed activities, and observations relevant to the research purpose. Explain the connection between each activity, the information it is expected to provide, and the study's objective.

Derive the content from the actual study. The document must accommodate different methodologies and study topics without requiring a particular task, questionnaire, manipulation, comparison, grouping arrangement, response type, or outcome measure.

Describe the intended participation setting and practical constraints when established. Record supplied requirements concerning equipment, presentation media, input methods, duration, or other conditions that shape the design. Do not assign default quantities, timing values, study conditions, or deployment choices.

## 3. Preserve the status of conceptual decisions

Represent established requirements and provisional proposals accurately. When the researcher is considering alternatives, describe them as alternatives and identify the decision still needed. Ask for clarification when an ambiguity prevents an accurate statement of the purpose, proposed activity, or intended observation.

Use Section VII for genuine decisions carried into design development. Later design details do not have to be settled during conceptualization. A completed concept must still explain the proposed study sufficiently to begin that development.

## 4. Check the concept

Confirm that the document explains the research purpose, what participants will do, what information will be obtained, and why that information is relevant. Check that it preserves the researcher's requirements and distinguishes proposed choices from established ones.

</response_requirement>

<response_format>

Save the completed document as CONCEPT.md at the experiment root. Use the outline below. Replace bracketed instructions with actual study content. Expand activity entries as needed and use complete sentences. Retain the main headings; omit conditional subentries and Section VII when they have no applicable content. Do not fill absent information with assumed study requirements.

~~~markdown
# Study concept

## I. Research purpose

[State the research question or assessment objective and the scope of the proposed study.]

## II. Background and relevant concepts

[Explain the supplied theoretical or practical background and the relevant concepts or behaviors. Include established hypotheses or expected relationships when applicable, and identify references used to support the account.]

## III. Intended participants

[Describe the intended population and any established participation requirements relevant to the concept.]

## IV. Proposed participant activities

### Activity [number]: [Name]

- **Activity:** [Describe what participants are proposed to do or experience.]
- **Purpose:** [Explain how the activity relates to the research objective.]
- **Intended observations:** [Describe the information expected from the activity.]

## V. Relationship between observations and purpose

[Explain how the proposed observations address the research question or assessment objective. Describe relationships across activities when relevant.]

## VI. Execution setting and practical constraints

[Describe the intended setting and established constraints that shape the study. Identify provisional choices as provisional.]

## VII. Decisions carried into design development

[Describe actual unresolved choices and any alternatives under consideration. Include this section when such decisions remain.]
~~~

The completed concept provides the basis for Stage 2 using [Design Doc Schematics.md](<Design Doc Schematics.md>). These preparation instructions remain in this specification and are not copied into CONCEPT.md.

</response_format>
