# Screen Study Materials

Apply the Designer perspective to material selection in Stage 3 of the [experiment workflow](<../../../Behavioral Experiment Workflow.md>).

## Establish the criteria and candidates

Read the supplied collection, provenance information, relevant design, and explicit screening requirements. Identify the candidate identities and the criteria applicable to individual items and to the collection as a whole.

Determine how each criterion can be evaluated from the available content, measurements, annotations, or source evidence. Preserve supplied definitions, thresholds, units, and decision rules. Ask the researcher to resolve absent or ambiguous criteria when they affect selection; do not invent scientific cutoffs or use an unstated quality standard.

Keep original source files intact. For a revised screening request, read the earlier selection record and identify which criteria or candidates have changed.

## Evaluate the evidence

- Inspect the actual content relevant to each criterion. Read text, inspect images, and review audio or video using available capabilities appropriate to the material. Filenames and descriptions alone cannot establish properties of uninspected content.
- Measure technical properties using suitable tools when a criterion requires measurement. Record the measurement method and units needed to interpret the result.
- Attribute supplied ratings, annotations, or published norms to their source. Keep them distinguishable from measurements made during screening and from qualitative judgments.
- Record what is established and what remains unknown. When a required property cannot be inspected or verified, leave the affected criterion unresolved rather than treating missing evidence as a pass or failure.
- If a criterion requires human ratings or other evidence not available from AI inspection, identify the required evidence. An AI judgment must not be presented as a human rating or a validated norm.

Assign accepted, excluded, or unresolved status using the researcher's rules and criterion-level findings. Acceptance requires adequate evidence for all applicable required criteria. Preserve the reason for each exclusion and the specific unresolved question for each unresolved item.

## Check the collection

Evaluate specified counts, categories, relationships, balance, or other collection-level requirements after item-level screening. Individual eligibility and inclusion in a constrained final collection may differ; record that distinction when it arises.

Use a supplied rule for choosing among eligible candidates. If the final selection depends on an unspecified tie-break or sampling decision, resolve it with the researcher. Preserve intentional repetitions and meaningful associations. Do not change criteria, duplicate items, or discard associated content merely to meet a quantity requirement.

## Deliver the selection

Produce a selection record in the requested or established project format. Identify each candidate by a stable source identifier or path, connect its status to the applicable criteria and evidence, and provide the selected collection or references to its members. Report collection-level requirements that remain unmet.

Use [Experiment File Structure.md](<../../../workflow-templates/Experiment File Structure.md>) when saving or organizing project outputs, and update the existing `LIB.md` for added locations. Preserve provenance and original sources; exclusion from the selected set does not require deletion.

Check that every candidate claimed as screened has actually been evaluated within the stated scope and that the selected set matches the decision record. A limited review must identify its coverage. Material editing and formatting follow their own tasks and may require screening again when relevant properties change.
