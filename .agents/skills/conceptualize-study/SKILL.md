# Conceptualize Study

Apply the Conceptualizer perspective to Stage 1 of the [experiment workflow](<../../../Behavioral Experiment Workflow.md>).

## Read the output specification

Read [Concept Doc Schematics.md](<../../../workflow-templates/Concept Doc Schematics.md>) before creating or revising the concept. Follow its output headings, conditional sections, and distinction between conceptual requirements and later design decisions. Resolve this resource relative to this skill's directory.

Use the selected research direction, supplied references, established session decisions, and available project documents. For revisions, read the existing `CONCEPT.md` first and preserve unaffected content. Equivalent user-supplied requirements can support creation from scratch.

## Establish the concept

- State the research question or assessment objective and its intended scope in the researcher's terms.
- Explain the relevant background and concepts using supplied or verified sources. Distinguish intended observations from hypotheses about what those observations will show.
- Describe the intended participants and the activities they are proposed to perform or experience.
- Connect each activity to its intended observations and explain how the observations address the research purpose. Avoid claiming that an observation establishes a construct or causal relationship beyond what the design and evidence support.
- Preserve established execution settings and practical constraints. Keep unselected alternatives provisional and retain exact requirements already supplied by the researcher.

Clarify ambiguity that prevents an accurate account of the study's purpose, proposed activity, or intended observation. Leave genuinely later design decisions in the specification's permitted decisions section. Do not choose an unprovided sample size, timing value, measure, grouping scheme, or deployment setting to make the document appear complete.

When relevant factual claims require verification, inspect accessible primary sources using available research capabilities. Preserve the boundary between verified background, researcher-supplied premises, and proposed interpretations. No particular methodology or set of activities is required by this skill.

## Write and verify

Write `CONCEPT.md` at the established experiment project root, or at the user's explicitly requested destination. The workflow toolkit's own root is not automatically the experiment destination. If the target project is ambiguous, resolve that location before saving.

Populate the specification's outline with actual content. Remove its instructional placeholders, repeat activity entries only as needed, and include the optional decisions section only for real unresolved choices. Keep preparation instructions outside the completed concept.

Check that the document explains what the study is for, what participants will do, what information it will obtain, and why that information is relevant. Confirm that provisional proposals remain distinguishable from established requirements and that source claims are supported.

Return a link to the saved document and identify any substantive conceptual choices still carried into design. If the user requests a draft in the conversation, deliver that form. The resulting concept provides the input for detailed experiment design; no implementation or other workflow artifacts are required by this skill alone.
