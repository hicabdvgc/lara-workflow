# Brainstorm Study

Apply the Conceptualizer perspective to Stage 1 of the [experiment workflow](<../../../Behavioral Experiment Workflow.md>). Produce candidate ideas and clarify the researcher's choices.

## Establish the direction

Read the user's request, relevant conversation decisions, and any supplied concept, notes, or references. Work from these inputs even when no project documents exist. Identify the research interest, established constraints, and the aspect the user wants to explore.

Ask a focused question when ambiguity prevents a useful interpretation of the requested direction. Use requirements already supplied in the session; avoid making the researcher repeat them. Keep questions about later implementation out of an early discussion unless they materially affect the current ideas.

## Develop and compare ideas

- Propose alternatives that differ in ways relevant to the user's question. Match their number and depth to the request.
- Explain each candidate's research question or assessment purpose, proposed participant activity, intended observations, and how those observations relate to the purpose.
- Describe practical implications under the supplied constraints. Identify tradeoffs when they help the researcher compare candidates.
- Present proposed activities, conditions, populations, and measures as proposals. Preserve established requirements and explain any suggested change to them.
- Keep methods open to the actual research purpose. Introduce comparisons, groups, manipulations, instruments, or quantitative measures when they serve the proposed study; do not treat them as universal requirements.
- Support claims about prior findings or established methods with sources actually inspected. Use available research tools when evidence is needed, and distinguish a creative proposal from a literature-supported claim.

## Refine through discussion

Incorporate the researcher's feedback into the candidates. Retain decisions already made and revise the alternatives affected by new constraints. Recommendations should explain their basis without representing a recommended option as the user's selection.

Record selected, rejected, and open directions only when those statuses follow from the conversation. Unselected ideas remain open unless the researcher has rejected them.

## Deliver

Return the requested discussion, comparison, or saved output. For an ordinary brainstorming request, present the ideas in the conversation; no project file is required. Include the current decisions and unresolved choice when applicable.

Before delivering, check that the alternatives address the stated direction, distinguish proposals from evidence, and preserve the researcher's constraints. Consolidation into `CONCEPT.md` is a separate requested outcome supported by `conceptualize-study`; detailed design follows once the direction is established.
