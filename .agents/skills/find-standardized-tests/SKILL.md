# Find Standardized Tests

Apply the Designer perspective to instrument discovery and comparison. Use the researcher's construct and assessment purpose to determine relevance.

## Establish the selection criteria

Read the user's requirements and relevant concept or design information when available. Identify the intended construct, population, language, administration setting, and any supplied constraints on duration, response mode, scoring, or access.

Clarify missing information when it changes which instruments could meet the request. If the user requests an exploratory survey of options, describe their differing applicability without assuming a target population or language. A completed design document is not a prerequisite for this task.

## Locate candidate instruments

Search official instrument resources, developer or publisher documentation, manuals available for inspection, and primary development or validation reports. Use secondary catalogs to discover candidates, then verify their claims in the relevant primary sources.

Keep instrument identity precise. Track the name, version, form, language, and adaptation being evaluated. Distinguish the original instrument from short forms, translations, revisions, and other adaptations.

## Verify suitability

For each candidate, establish the following information as relevant and available:

- Identify its stated purpose and intended population, with the source supporting that applicability.
- Describe the administration requirements and available scoring information, including the meaning of the resulting scores. Attribute duration, item counts, score ranges, norms, and interpretation rules to the specific version inspected.
- Summarize validation or reliability evidence relevant to the proposed use. Identify the population, language, administration mode, and version studied. Separate evidence reported by the developer from independent evidence when that distinction is relevant.
- Identify the official access location and any documented requirements for obtaining, administering, reproducing, translating, or digitally presenting the instrument. Inspect current official information when availability or usage conditions affect the recommendation.
- Compare the instrument against the researcher's criteria. Explain mismatches and information that remains unavailable instead of treating silence as evidence of suitability.

Do not transfer norms or validation claims across versions, populations, languages, or administration modes without supporting evidence. A proposed change to an instrument must remain identifiable as an adaptation. Scoring or interpretation details behind an inaccessible manual remain unverified.

## Deliver a comparison

Return a source-linked comparison in the requested format. Identify each candidate's exact version, relevant evidence, administration and scoring information, access location, fit to the criteria, and unresolved selection questions. A table is useful when the same properties can be meaningfully compared across candidates.

Provide a recommendation when requested, with its basis and the limits of the inspected evidence. The researcher decides which instrument to adopt. If no verified candidate meets the criteria, explain the specific mismatch rather than substituting an invented or modified test as a standardized one.

Deliver links and appropriately limited descriptions of protected instruments; obtaining access or reproducing test content requires the relevant authorization. Do not contact providers or purchase materials as an implicit part of discovery.

Before delivery, check source links, version identities, and the correspondence between each evidence claim and the candidate to which it is attributed. Return the comparison in the conversation unless the user requests a saved artifact. Preserve instrument identity and verified administration requirements for subsequent design and material collection.
