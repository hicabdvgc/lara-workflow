# Create Study Materials

Apply the Designer perspective, with Conceptualizer support when needed to connect the content to the study purpose. Supply original materials for Stages 3 and 5 of the [experiment workflow](<../../../Behavioral Experiment Workflow.md>).

## Read the content brief

Read the requested material types, their role in the study, content and quantity requirements, and intended output formats. Use the relevant design or equivalent explicit user requirements. Reference examples can inform the requested style or structure without establishing additional scientific requirements.

Clarify missing decisions that determine item meaning, quantities, conditions, labels, or other experimentally meaningful properties. Distinguish constraints the researcher has established from candidate creative choices that the task leaves open. Do not invent a study design to make an underspecified material brief executable.

Identify whether the request covers original source material, completed experiment assets, or both. Use [Experiment File Structure.md](<../../../workflow-templates/Experiment File Structure.md>) for project locations and source preservation. When producing completed assets, also read the relevant requirements in [Template Schematics.md](<../../../workflow-templates/Template Schematics.md>) and the experiment's actual asset structures. Resolve references relative to this skill directory.

## Author the requested content

- Create the required text, images, audio, video, or other materials using appropriate available authoring capabilities and their applicable instructions. Produce actual usable files in the requested formats.
- Preserve the brief's language, content relationships, quantities, meaningful order, and presentation constraints. Keep associated parts of an item together and use the project's identifiers where established.
- Track which newly authored source belongs to each required item or collection. Retain an identifiable original version so later screening, formatting, and revisions can be traced to it.
- Derive labels, answer keys, or annotations from the brief's specified rules. Clarify ambiguous scientific judgments instead of treating generated content as its own validation evidence.
- Keep preparation notes and agent-facing commentary outside participant-facing materials. Record authorship and creation context in the appropriate project record when needed for traceability.

Use available tooling within its actual capabilities. If a requested modality cannot be created or inspected, identify the specific missing capability and the affected output. Avoid substituting another kind of material without an established requirement.

## Check the output against the brief

Compare the actual materials with the content, quantity, relationship, and format requirements. Inspect rendered or played media, and check technical properties where the brief specifies them. For structured text or collections, verify syntax, identities, associated files, and required content.

Distinguish directly checked properties from judgments that require screening or empirical evidence. Newly authored items are original materials; do not describe them as a published instrument, standardized test, validated stimulus set, or empirical finding without the corresponding basis. An intended content category or difficulty level does not itself establish a measured property.

When screening or formatting is included in the request, use [screen-study-materials](../screen-study-materials/SKILL.md) or [format-study-materials](../format-study-materials/SKILL.md) for the applicable handoff. Preserve original sources when preparing derivatives.

## Deliver

Save original materials under the established `sources/` arrangement or the requested destination, and completed assets under `assets/` when that work is requested. Update affected references and the existing `LIB.md`. Return the files with their source identities, a concise account of conformity to the brief, and any outstanding screening or verification.
