# Plan Study Instructions

Apply the Designer perspective to instruction planning in Stage 2 and material preparation in the [experiment workflow](<../../../Behavioral Experiment Workflow.md>).

## Establish the instruction requirements

Read the study procedure, relevant requirements, the researcher's intended instructions, and any existing wording or briefing materials. Identify the audience, delivery setting, and requested output format. Equivalent explicit user requirements can support planning without a completed design document.

Work with the researcher to determine what the audience needs to know to perform the specified activities. Consider actions, response meanings, progression, and completion where they are relevant to the study. Identify the points in the existing procedure at which information must be available and any design-defined differences among conditions or participant groups.

Clarify decisions that affect what instructions reveal, what participants are asked to do, or how their responses are interpreted. Keep proposed wording or delivery changes distinguishable from established requirements. Do not introduce practice, comprehension checks, feedback, or additional procedural steps without support in the researcher's decisions.

## Develop content and delivery

For each needed instruction, establish whom it addresses, what it communicates, when it is provided, and whether it appears within the experiment or in separate material. Develop complete wording consistent with the actual response controls, task rules, terminology, and participant-facing labels. Preserve supplied wording where the researcher requires it.

Describe applicable presentation and progression requirements, including conditions for showing or leaving instructions, when these have been established. If wording depends on an unresolved design choice, resolve that choice before finalizing the affected content. Routine editorial choices can follow the requested style without changing the procedure.

Keep the plan proportionate to the request. It can be expressed in the relevant design sections and a concise delivery summary; a separate planning document is needed only when requested or required by the project's organization.

## Record embedded instructions

Read [Design Doc Schematics.md](<../../../workflow-templates/Design Doc Schematics.md>), resolving the reference relative to this skill directory. Create or revise the relevant portions of `DESIGN.md` at the experiment root.

Record instruction placement in the procedure and their content, presentation, participant actions, and progression requirements in the relevant component breakdown. Preserve the rest of the existing design. When creating a design document for a limited instruction-planning request, keep unspecified study decisions identifiable rather than inventing a complete experiment.

Embedded instruction requirements proceed through the existing asset preparation and experiment-building capabilities when implementation is requested. This planning skill supplies the content and requirements for that work.

## Prepare separate materials

Create separate briefing or instruction materials in the user's requested format using the appropriate available document or media capability. Identify their audience and relationship to the specified procedure. Keep researcher-facing preparation notes outside material intended for participants unless the requested format calls for them.

Save the deliverables in the established study location, maintain affected references, and update the existing `LIB.md` when needed.

## Verify and deliver

Check wording against the intended actions, controls, conditions, and completion rules. Inspect the rendered or played separate material as appropriate to its format. Return the instruction plan, affected `DESIGN.md` content, and requested separate files, identifying any unresolved decisions accurately.

The researcher determines the instruction requirements and manages human testing. This skill prepares instructions; it does not guide testers during execution or organize test repetitions. Claims about comprehension require supplied human observations.
